\begin{abstract}
Cross-map comparison of Counter-Strike 2 defensive openings is complicated by map geometry, resources, and recurring teams. We study window representations, source-fitted deployment patterns, and evidence retrieval without assigning fixed tactical duties to locations. An observable schema combines coarse geography, resources, relative height, ground state, and sampled motion with explicit masks. Graph and nongraph encoders, with and without recurrence, share a whole-window reconstruction objective and consistent player matching. A development matrix covers 465 windows from nine series and six maps. Frozen-checkpoint confirmation adds 246 windows from 12 newly acquired series, with two series per map and no target-side training. The recurrent graph model has a geometric mean loss ratio of 1.016 relative to its full-input nongraph recurrent control, with a model-based 95 percent interval of 0.996 to 1.036. A prespecified variance sensitivity also includes parity. Joint-context clusters have high development stability, but equipment dominates their centroid separation. Existing human returns support only a small restricted-candidate-pool replay. The results bound architectural and pattern-interpretation claims; independent retrieval relevance and practical coaching efficacy remain unconfirmed. % [claim:C180] [evidence:E110,E124,E126,E131]
\end{abstract}

Counter-Strike 2, defensive deployments, representation learning, cross-map generalization, graph models.

\section{Introduction}
Representing a Counter-Strike 2 defensive opening across maps requires a distinction between a player's map-specific location and the deployment information that a representation is intended to retain. Similar occupancy counts or short Euclidean distances alone do not establish that two deployments support the same defensive interpretation.

TAGNet studies defensive pattern recognition from five CT snapshots on Dust2~\cite{tagnet2026}. Our research concerns the extension of deployment representations, pattern analysis, and comparable-opening retrieval across maps. Snapshot reconstruction, pattern stability, retrieval relevance, and next-state prediction answer different questions. The latter is retained as an auxiliary probe.

\subsection{Research questions and intended contribution}
We organize the study around four questions: which deployment information a common representation retains across maps (R1); what graph structure and learned temporal aggregation contribute under matched inputs (R2); whether discovered patterns are stable and retrieved deployments are interpretable and relevant (R3); and whether the frozen procedure transfers to independent series under a stated map-exclusion regime (R4).

Our contribution is an empirical framework for cross-map CT opening analysis. It combines an observation and missingness contract, source-fitted architecture and pattern comparisons under matched targets, and separate interventions on representation distance and observed context. The contribution is the integrated task definition, controls and transfer evidence. Graph encoders, recurrence and contextual features are established components; the new-series comparison does not establish graph superiority, and branch separation does not imply statistically disentangled representations. % [claim:C183] [evidence:E101,E107,E124,E130,E131]

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Defensive pattern representation}
TAGNet is the reference for the original snapshot-reconstruction and defensive-pattern task~\cite{tagnet2026}. We distinguish its feature vocabulary, context branch, aggregation implementation, and clustering analysis from the present common-input and learned-temporal variants.

The released author manuscript explicitly keeps its GRU and context projection at seeded initialization because snapshot reconstruction bypasses them; it fits its reported clustering to embeddings from all reported rounds. Our window decoder instead trains the temporal path, and development centroids are fitted only on source-training embeddings. These are changes in objective and evaluation scope, not repairs to an unintended missing-gradient defect. Absolute reconstruction losses across the two tasks are not directly comparable. % [claim:C206] [evidence:E205,E107,E111]

\subsection{Permutation-invariant and graph controls}
Deep Sets develops a framework for permutation-invariant set functions~\cite{NIPS2017_f22e4747}. Janossy pooling constructs invariance through averaging an order-sensitive function over reorderings~\cite{murphy2019janossy}. Our existing five-player non-graph control applies this symmetrization principle to jointly permuted nodes and edge endpoints. It serves as a comparator and is not presented as a new pooling algorithm.

\subsection{Team observation, movement and spatial context}
X-Ego learns team-level video representations and evaluates teammate/opponent location prediction; its reported dataset uses Mirage and a round-based partition~\cite{wang2025xego}. Our offline CT-window task uses structured team observations, rather than enforcing individual first-person visibility, and addresses a different endpoint. % [claim:C201] [evidence:E201,E103]

Durst et al. learn movement control for CS:GO Retakes on Dust2~\cite{durst2024move}. Human-like movement generation is distinct from retrieving comparable observed openings. Their map-specific training design does not supply a zero-shot multi-map result for the present task. % [claim:C202] [evidence:E202,E109]

Xenopoulos et al. derive directed navigation-graph distances and contextual action values~\cite{xenopoulos2020valuing}. Their spatial example distinguishes asymmetric traversal from Euclidean proximity. Our current geometry is therefore described as an observation, not a validated navigation or tactical-role relation. % [claim:C203] [evidence:E203,E101]

\subsection{Generalization protocols and cluster interpretation}
Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz emphasize the role of architecture, experimental conditions and model selection in domain-generalization comparisons~\cite{gulrajani2020domain}. WILDS organizes evaluation around explicit real-world distribution shifts~\cite{koh2021wilds}. We accordingly distinguish unseen series, training-held maps and chronological transfer; newly acquired data alone do not establish a future-only test. % [claim:C207] [evidence:E206,E207]

Von Luxburg's overview distinguishes stability mechanisms under idealized and actual K-means optimization~\cite{luxburg2010stability}. Its assumptions matter when interpreting agreement across fits. Our stability diagnostics characterize a chosen observation space; an interpretation as defensive duties requires additional evidence. % [claim:C208] [evidence:E208]

Graded cumulative-gain evaluation accommodates nonbinary relevance judgments~\cite{jarvelin2002gain}. Buckley and Voorhees examine the sensitivity of evaluation to incomplete judgments~\cite{buckley2004incomplete}. We therefore describe the old rated-subset replay as a conditional diagnostic, and do not infer full-library relevance from it. The exponential gain and missing-value rules are specified separately in our protocol. % [claim:C209] [evidence:E210,E211]

This is a targeted narrative comparison of game representations, source-domain selection, clustering stability and graded retrieval evaluation. It motivates separating computational transfer, context-conditioned grouping and useful tactical reference. It does not establish that the component techniques are new or that the literature set is exhaustive.

\section{Common Deployment Representation}
\subsection{Recorded common-input baseline}
The current common-input implementation uses five place categories: BombsiteA, BombsiteB, CTSpawn, TSpawn, and other. Each node contains their one-hot encoding, normalized health, transformed equipment value, and an alive indicator, giving eight channels. This coarse vocabulary is not a validated tactical-role taxonomy.

The existing auxiliary state contains the five live-category fractions, dead-player fraction, and mean and maximum live-pair Euclidean distances. Distances use a fixed 5,000-game-unit scale. Undefined pair statistics are masked.

\subsection{Implemented geometric descriptors}
The v0.3 model input includes relative height, airborne/grounded/unknown state, horizontal distance, height difference, and validity masks. NAV-candidate and patch-matching diagnostics remain excluded from the model. These fields describe observations and do not establish tactical semantics.

The representation is an observable feature interface, not a latent tactical-duty annotation. Similar recorded windows may differ in visibility, opponents or unobserved intent. The separate context facets expose additional information without converting a location into a universal role.


\subsection{Implemented node and pair contract}
The implemented input combines the original eight node fields with relative height, ground-state descriptors and two sampled-motion quantities, producing 15 node channels and three pair channels; NAV projection features are excluded from the model input. % [claim:C101] [evidence:E101,E103]
The input contract supplies no fixed place-to-duty vocabulary or duty-label array. Place membership describes geography; tactical interpretation is conditional on the particular deployment window and available context, with multiple interpretations or insufficient evidence allowed. % [claim:C102] [evidence:E101,E103]
Death masks zero invalid nodes and pairs. Ground-state feature masks distinguish physical observations from an explicit unknown indicator; raw place names remain available for traceability. These observations drive the reported representation learning without assigning duties. % [claim:C103] [evidence:E101,E103,E107]

\paragraph{Information boundary}
The joined encoder observations are five ordered snapshots of coarse geographic membership, survival, health, equipment, relative geometry, ground state and adjacent-sample displacement rates. Utility events, facing/visibility and round-level score/economy have not been joined to this input; missing context is not assumed known. % [claim:C108] [evidence:E101,E103]

\paragraph{Causal sampled motion}
For each player, the two appended quantities are horizontal displacement magnitude and signed vertical displacement between adjacent supplied observations, divided by elapsed seconds and a fixed scale of 250 game units per second. Identities align endpoints even when player order changes. Both endpoints must be alive; the first observation and invalid intervals are zero-filled and masked, whereas observed stationarity remains a valid zero. Only the current and preceding observations are used. The scale is not a speed bound, and values are not clipped. These quantities retain neither the intervening path nor horizontal movement direction. % [claim:C110] [evidence:E101,E103]

\subsection{Asset and navigation validity}
Patch-number agreement alone does not establish exact map-asset compatibility. NAV residuals, traversable distances and visibility require their respective evidence; structural validity is separate from context-specific tactical interpretation.
Only the first five height/ground quantities enter the deployment node input; NAV-candidate, patch-matching and ray diagnostics remain excluded. Native ground-state observations and validity masks are preserved separately from navigation diagnostics. The present results make no claim of validated walkability, connectivity or visibility from a NAV projection. % [claim:C185] [evidence:E101,E103,E129]

\subsection{Observed context and branch separation}
The separate context vector contains five log-transformed CT equipment totals, the two pre-round public scores and thirty log-transformed CT utility counts: six types at each of the five supplied times. Counts start at freeze end and stop at the current query time. A missing required group makes that context policy unsupported, while policies using available groups remain evaluable. These are recorded observations, not active utility areas or inferred intentions. % [claim:C215] [evidence:E118,E121,E140,E147]

The node representation already includes individual equipment. Consequently, adding equipment context repeats an aggregate view of information partly present in the encoder; the branches are not disjoint information sources. Our controls vary the retrieval or clustering contribution of explicit context. They do not enforce resource-invariant embeddings, orthogonality or statistical independence, and the term deployment representation does not assert that equipment information has been removed. % [claim:C216] [evidence:E103,E107,E147]

\section{Models and Learning Procedure}
\subsection{Common notation}
For each window, let $X_t$, $A_t$, and $M_t$ denote node observations, pair information, and masks. Let $H_{t,u}$ be the encoded state of a player track or a consistently ordered whole-window permutation view. Temporal encoding precedes invariant pooling:
\begin{equation}
 z=\frac{1}{U}\sum_{u=1}^{U}\mathrm{GRU}_{\phi}(H_{1,u},\ldots,H_{5,u})_5.
\end{equation}
For the graph condition, $U=5$ player tracks; for the non-graph condition, $U=120$ consistent joint node/edge permutations. The static counterparts average the same encoded states over time and views without a recurrent module. Player identity aligns tracks but is not an encoder feature. % [claim:C120] [evidence:E107]

\subsection{Implemented common decoder and matching objective}
All six conditions share a 64-to-128-to-525 decoder: five times by five anonymous player slots by 15 node channels, plus five times by ten unordered player pairs by three pair channels. The reconstruction loss averages seven groups: survival binary cross-entropy, live-player coarse-region cross-entropy, resource MSE, relative-height MSE, ground-state cross-entropy, known-motion MSE and valid-pair MSE. Each group averages its valid entries, with an empty group contributing zero. A single jointly permuted player assignment across all times and pair endpoints minimizes the window loss over 120 assignments. Survival, region and ground outputs are logits; continuous outputs remain unconstrained regressions. % [claim:C121] [evidence:E107]
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}_i=\min_{\pi\in S_5}\frac{1}{7}
 \sum_{g=1}^{7}\ell_g(\widehat{Y}_i,\pi\!\cdot\!Y_i;\pi\!\cdot\!M_i).
\end{equation}
Here the same $\pi$ acts on every supplied time and both pair endpoints; independently matching each frame would change the task.

\subsection{Reference and comparison families}
The implemented development comparison includes direct common-feature sequences, a full-input non-graph encoder, a distance-aware graph encoder, and corresponding static and learned-temporal conditions. Original TAGNet context and fixed aggregation are described separately; the revised matrix does not reproduce that complete context branch.

The completed v17 non-graph control reads the full masked node matrix, distance matrix, and alive mask. It averages a shared MLP over all 120 joint player permutations, preserving edge-endpoint correspondence in every input. Static and causal-GRU prediction variants were compared under the recorded development recipe.

All six conditions were trained from scratch under the fixed development recipe: AdamW with learning rate $10^{-3}$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size 16, 100 epochs, gradient clipping at 5, and seeds 0, 1 and 42. The earliest epoch minimizing source-validation reconstruction was selected. Nine series-exclusion folds and six map-plus-series-exclusion folds yielded 270 selected checkpoints; pilots were excluded. The graph and MLP models share input observations, 64-dimensional bottlenecks and the decoder, but their parameter counts are not exactly matched. % [claim:C122] [evidence:E109,E110]

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Development and final isolation}
Recorded G3 prediction controls leave one complete series out. Recorded G4 controls exclude a target map and every series containing that map from training and validation. These are development results. The new-series technical protocol reuses the corresponding G4 source models and libraries, with all new series excluded from those libraries; this is a local freeze rather than an external preregistration.

\subsection{Pattern and retrieval endpoints}
\paragraph{Source-normalized context intervention}
For a source vector group of dimension $d$, let $a$ be its mean squared Euclidean distance over distinct source pairs. Centering by the source mean and dividing by $\sqrt{a}$ gives unit mean source-pair distance; numerically constant groups use denominator $\sqrt{d}$. Context groups are scaled separately and receive equal weight within the selected group set $G$. Thus the ranking distance is % [claim:C219] [evidence:E121,E147]
\begin{equation}
 D_\lambda(q,s)=(1-\lambda)d_z(q,s)
 +\frac{\lambda}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}
 \|\widetilde{c}_{g,q}-\widetilde{c}_{g,s}\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
The same source-only scaling applies to embedding distances. Statistics first use their recorded source-fitted feature standardization; complete observation matching normalizes its permutation-minimized distance by the mean distinct-source-pair distance. Joint clustering concatenates the correspondingly square-root-weighted vectors. Context-only drops the representation term. All means and scales are source-fitted, including when query availability changes. % [claim:C220] [evidence:E121,E123,E147]

The development pattern-analysis implementation uses scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011sklearn}. Candidate cluster counts are two through six, selected solely by source-training silhouette; the query embeddings do not select or refit centroids. Numeric cluster IDs are retained without tactical names. A source-radius and minimum-support rule records rejected queries as a diagnostic, with cross-seed agreement reported only where both assignments are accepted and vary across at least two clusters. The separate source-series bootstrap fixes the neural encoder and selected cluster count. % [claim:C204] [evidence:E111,E112,E204]

Transfer reuses the saved source centroids, cluster support and 95th-percentile source radius with minimum support five. It does not refit on new windows. We report both coverage and cross-seed adjusted Rand index on common accepted windows, requiring multiple clusters in each assignment. Eligibility can differ between methods, so their ARI summaries are conditional diagnostics. Source-series bootstrap variability fixes the encoder and source-selected cluster count. % [claim:C186] [evidence:E111,E114,E134,E137]

The old-pair replay uses graded discounted gain, with cutoff $\min(5,N)$ and the ideal ranking restricted to the same eligible rated pool. Its discounted-gain definition is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{DCG@\min(5,N)}=\sum_{k=1}^{\min(5,N)}
 \frac{2^{r_k}-1}{\log_2(k+1)}.
\end{equation}
Grades for an exact pair are averaged before ranking evaluation; missing or unjudgeable values are not zero. Singleton and all-zero-gain pools are undefined. Seeds are averaged within query before query macro-averaging. These candidate-pool scores do not establish full-library recall or new-series relevance.

\subsection{Sampling units, uncertainty, and selection}
The new-series technical comparison uses series as its analysis unit. Frames, rounds and seeds are aggregated within series rather than counted as additional independent series. Existing development prediction intervals are conditional descriptive series-resampling summaries; they do not remove shared-team or overlapping-training dependencies.

For map $m$ and its two series $j$, let $r_{mj}$ be the log ratio of the series-averaged Graph+GRU and MLP+GRU losses. We estimate the equal-map mean and its residual standard error as % [claim:C218] [evidence:E131,E132]
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mu}=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{m=1}^{6}\overline{r}_m,
 \qquad
 \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{72}\sum_{m=1}^{6}s_m^2}.
\end{equation}
The common-variance interval uses six residual degrees of freedom. The prespecified unequal-variance sensitivity changes the critical value using Welch--Satterthwaite degrees of freedom, retaining the same point estimate and standard error. These model-based intervals assume independent normal series residuals; they are not design-based guarantees.
The primary external quantity is the equal-map mean of series log loss ratios after averaging windows and seeds within each series. A pooled within-map normal-residual t interval has N minus six residual degrees of freedom; the prespecified variance sensitivity uses the map-specific variance contributions and Welch--Satterthwaite approximation. Neither procedure resolves shared-team/event dependence. Other reconstruction conditions and retrieval/pattern diagnostics are descriptive; no target-side model, policy or sample-size selection is performed. % [claim:C187] [evidence:E129,E131,E132]

The G4 Nuke diagnostic contains 62 windows from three series. Full Graph + GRU has 8.44\% higher loss than MLP + GRU and 3.72\% higher loss than its common-input control. These post-hoc component differences use the fixed CE/MSE scales and model-specific optimal player matchings; they do not identify a causal input-field failure. % [claim:C146] [evidence:E117]

A separate context audit reread the same 22 development demos without changing any encoder input. It recovered pre-round scores for all 465 windows, CT and T equipment totals at all 2,325 query times, and finite angle pairs for all 11,368 live CT observations. Dead-player angle slots are masked. All six queries are first-round windows under the original first-in-index selection rule, so their resource conditions are not representative of the full cohort. \section{Discussion and Limitations}
\subsection{What the evidence can establish}
Existing predictive controls help separate raw-input availability from architecture choice. Euclidean geometry and pooled occupancy cannot, by themselves, certify tactical responsibilities, walkable routes, or useful deployment analogies. The current development cohort is small and contains repeated teams.

A synthetic information-loss probe preserves coarse place categories, resources and relative formation while translating the whole team over time. The preceding 13-channel node and three-channel pair summaries remain identical, while the appended motion quantities distinguish the constructed moving and stationary windows. Endpoint displacement still cannot recover an intervening out-and-back path or identify horizontal direction. This diagnostic checks information retention, not real tactical intent or model accuracy. % [claim:C109] [evidence:E101,E103]

The new-series reconstruction estimate is consistent with the development warning against assuming a graph advantage. Temporal controls remain informative, while additional motion channels do not automatically improve this objective. In a separate development analysis, the highly stable joint clusters largely follow resources. These results motivate retaining deployment observations, contextual facets, and tactical judgments as separate evidence layers; no fixed place-to-duty rule follows from the current experiments. % [claim:C181] [evidence:E110,E124,E131]

\subsection{Answers to the research questions}
R1 is supported at the level of a computational observation contract: the input preserves the declared geometry, validity and endpoint-motion distinctions. It does not encode complete routes or tactical duties. For R2, the matched reconstruction comparisons support examining temporal aggregation, while the frozen technical confirmation gives no stable graph advantage under the stated objective. These conclusions concern this decoder, capacity choice and observation space.

For R3, source-radius coverage and seed agreement show properties of a chosen representation and rejection rule. A resource-driven partition can become more stable without becoming a better defensive taxonomy. The restricted old rated-pool replay does not establish that newly retrieved candidates are useful. R4 supplies a frozen new-series comparison with map exclusion at training time; shared teams, selected events and researcher familiarity with the maps limit broader transfer claims. The study therefore separates computational transfer from semantic or coaching validation.

\subsection{Selection, capacity and observation limitations}
The cohort is conditional on complete supplied windows and native observations. Excluding an early-ending window changes the population from arbitrary rounds to eligible openings; repeated windows and seeds do not compensate for the small number of series. The event-balanced sample is constrained by available metadata and archives, and two series per map provide little information about within-map residual distributions.

The graph and nongraph models share targets and a bottleneck, but their parameter counts and inductive biases differ. The comparison does not isolate topology as a causal factor, and permutation-averaging costs differ between the families. A low reconstruction loss can retain common resources or geography without preserving rare tactical distinctions. Endpoint motion, Euclidean geometry and global team observations also leave first-person visibility, intervening paths, opponent information and intent unresolved.

The context diagnosis concerns explicit weighting of an overlapping information branch. It does not demonstrate a resource-free latent space or select an optimal tactical weight. Missing telemetry, source-radius rejection and conditional ARI are separate filters; their retained populations should not be merged into a single accuracy or generalized to all openings.

\subsection{Scope of the project}
This CT study targets offline deployment representation and evidence retrieval. T-side openings, a production backend, coaching interventions, and win-rate effects are separate projects and have not been validated by the present experiments.

\section{Conclusion}
We report an auditable CT opening schema and matched architecture, pattern, and retrieval controls across maps. The frozen new-series technical comparison does not establish a stable graph advantage. Recorded context can stabilize clusters while changing what those clusters represent, so computational stability must not substitute for tactical interpretation. The limited existing judged-pool replay remains auxiliary; independent semantic retrieval and coaching efficacy are unresolved. % [claim:C182] [evidence:E124,E126,E131]
